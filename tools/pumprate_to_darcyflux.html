Darcy Flux Around a Well – Full Derivation

This calculation determines the radial Darcy flux by dividing the volumetric flow rate by the cylindrical flow area through which groundwater flows. As the radius increases, the cylindrical flow area becomes larger, causing the Darcy flux to decrease with distance.

Derivation

The volumetric flow rate is:

$$ Q\,[\text{m}^3/\text{day}] $$

The water flows through a cylindrical surface at radius:

$$ r = r_w + d $$

where:

  • $r_w$ is the well screen radius [m]
  • $d$ is the distance from the well screen surface [m]

The cylindrical flow area is:

$$ A(r) = 2\pi (r_w + d)\, b $$

Darcy flux is:

$$ q(d) = \frac{Q}{A(r)} $$

Final equation:

$$ q(d) = \frac{Q}{2\pi (r_w + d)\, b} $$
